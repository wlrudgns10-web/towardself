About the Whisperling Test

At everyone's ear lives a little devil that twists other people's words: the Whisperling. This is a place to see how often yours chimes in.

What is this site?

Someone only says "Let's talk about this tomorrow," yet you hear "I did something wrong." Something slipped in between what they said and what you heard. This site draws that "something" as a character called the Whisperling, and offers a free test to see how often, and in what way, your Whisperling chimes in. Along with your results, it suggests practices for not being pushed around by it.

Where does the Whisperling come from?

The Whisperling is a new character built by connecting three lines of thought. Read more in Who Is the Whisperling?

  • Carl Jung: projection, seeing in others the parts of ourselves we haven't accepted (the shadow) and the ideals we long for (anima/animus). This is what the Whisperling uses as fuel.
  • Phil Stutz and Barry Michels: "Part X," the name the two therapists gave in Coming Alive to the inner enemy that blocks our growth. The Whisperling reimagines this idea for hearing words in relationships. The tools matched to each type on the results page were inspired by their book The Tools.
  • Viktor Frankl: logotherapy, the idea that we can't choose our circumstances but can choose our attitude. It's the basis for the thought that even when the Whisperling chimes in, we can choose whether to believe it.

Please keep in mind

  • This test is not a clinical diagnostic tool. It is a self-understanding test that has not yet been validated.
  • It is a homemade scale built with reference to the ideas above, and it has not been studied for reliability or validity.
  • It is an unofficial personal project with no connection to Phil Stutz, Barry Michels, Carl Jung's estate or any related organization. When introducing their ideas, we describe them in our own words rather than quoting the original texts.
  • Please use your results only as prompts for self-reflection. If you've been struggling for a long time, we recommend talking with a mental health professional.
