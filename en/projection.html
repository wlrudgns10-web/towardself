Projection: When We See Ourselves in Others

The picture on a movie screen doesn't come from the screen. It comes from the projector. Sometimes what we see in other people works the same way.

What is projection?

Projection is when the mind experiences our own thoughts, feelings or traits as belonging to someone else. The term first came from psychoanalysis, and Jung believed the shadow in particular reveals itself through projection.

We don't project on purpose. It happens automatically, in an instant. That's why it's so hard to recognize projection while it's happening. We simply feel, "That person is just like that."

Three everyday faces of projection

1. Blame projection

We see our own frustration or aggression in the other person. A coworker's short reply reads as "they're ignoring me," or an ordinary suggestion feels like "they put me down." In the Whisperling Test, this is the Thorn Whisperling.

2. Self-critical projection

We shine our harsh inner critic onto other people's eyes. No one has blamed us, yet we feel "they all think I'm not good enough." It can look like modesty, but in fact we've invented a thought the other person never had. In the Whisperling Test, this is the Grader Whisperling.

3. Idealizing projection

We shine what we wish for onto someone else. A person we've just met feels like destiny, or we find special meaning in an ordinary remark from someone we admire. See Anima and Animus for more. In the Whisperling Test, this is the Halo Whisperling.

Signs you might be projecting

  • Your feeling is far bigger than the situation calls for.
  • You find yourself using words like "always," "never" or "that's just how they are."
  • Different people start to look the same ("Everyone uses me").
  • You feel certain about someone's intentions without ever having checked.

Seeing these signs doesn't mean your judgment is wrong. Some people really are rude. It just means it's worth asking once whether your feeling is information about them, or information about you.

What changes when you notice projection

The moment you notice a projection, the other person turns back from "a screen reflecting my mind" into "a separate person, different from me."

Needless misunderstandings and fights in the relationship become fewer. And when you realize that what you saw in someone else was also in you, you understand yourself more fully. That's why Jung saw projection as a clue to knowing ourselves.
