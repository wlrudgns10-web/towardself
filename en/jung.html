Who Was Carl Jung?

"Shadow," "persona," "introvert and extrovert." A surprising number of the psychology words we use every day came from one person.

A Swiss psychiatrist

Carl Gustav Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist. The son of a pastor, he studied medicine in Basel and then worked at a psychiatric hospital in Zurich, where he studied the human mind by spending years with patients. The school of psychology he founded is called analytical psychology.

Meeting Freud, and parting ways

As a young man, Jung was close to Sigmund Freud, the founder of psychoanalysis. For a while Freud even saw Jung as his heir. But in the early 1910s the two went separate ways.

  • Freud saw the depths of the mind mainly as a store of repressed desires, especially sexual ones.
  • Jung saw the unconscious not just as a storeroom for what we push down, but also as a source of wisdom and of forces that help us grow.

Because of this difference, Jungian psychology asks not only "What hurt me?" but also "Who am I becoming?"

Key ideas Jung left behind

  • The shadow: the parts of myself I have pushed away
  • The persona: the mask I wear in society
  • Anima and animus: the opposite side within me
  • The collective unconscious and archetypes: a shared foundation of the human mind. Jung believed this is why images like the hero, the mother and the wise old man keep returning in myths and dreams.
  • Introversion and extraversion: whether a person's mental energy tends to flow inward or outward. Many of today's popular personality-type tests were inspired by this idea.
  • The Self and individuation: the process of bringing all of this together and becoming a whole person

Something to keep in mind

Jung's ideas give us a rich language for understanding the mind, but many of them are hard to test experimentally. That is why psychology today often treats them less as scientific facts and more as lenses for self-reflection. The Whisperling Test was built in the same spirit.
