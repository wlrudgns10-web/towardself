Handling Your Whisperling with Logotherapy

We can't choose our circumstances, but we can choose our attitude toward them.

What is logotherapy?

Logotherapy is a form of therapy created by the Austrian psychiatrist Viktor Frankl (1905–1997). The Greek word logos here means "meaning." Frankl believed that what people want most deeply is not pleasure or power but meaning in life, and that people are strongest when they find it.

Frankl was held in concentration camps during the Second World War and survived. The book he wrote from that experience is still widely read. Logotherapy does not assume any particular religion.

Core ideas of logotherapy

  • Self-distancing: the ability to put a step between "me, the one thinking" and "the thought that came up." It means people are beings who can observe their own thoughts.
  • Freedom to choose your attitude: even in circumstances we can't change, we can choose how we face them.
  • Dereflection: looking at ourselves too closely can make problems bigger, so we turn our attention to the tasks and people in front of us.
  • Three paths to meaning: people find meaning when they do or create something, when they experience something or meet someone, and when they choose their attitude toward what can't be avoided.

Three steps for not being pushed around by your Whisperling

The practice on the Whisperling Test results page brings these ideas into everyday life. Here, the Whisperling stands for the inner interpretation that twists other people's words before they reach us.

1. Pause: name the whisper (self-distancing)

When you feel shaken, say to yourself before reacting: "Ah, my Whisperling is back." Treat the thought like a passing guest rather than a fact. You decide whether to open the door.

2. Separate: what was said vs. what was whispered (freedom to choose your attitude)

Split what the other person actually said and what the Whisperling added into two columns. What was said is a circumstance you can't choose. Whether to believe the whisper is an attitude you choose.

3. Choose: ask what you want to protect (meaning)

Ask, "Is this information about them, or about my own mind?" Then think, "What do I want to protect in this relationship?" Choose your response based on what matters to you rather than the feeling of the moment.

Daily review (5 minutes)

Five minutes before bed, write down these four things:

  1. The moment I was most shaken today
  2. What my Whisperling told me
  3. What actually happened
  4. One meaningful moment today

The last question matters. If you write only the moments that shook you, your day becomes a list of problems. Ending with a meaningful moment also shows you what you value, and that becomes your guide for choosing how to respond the next day.
