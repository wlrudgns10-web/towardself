Handling Your Inner Critic

Your manager only said, "Should we consider other options?" Yet in your head you hear, "See, you're not good enough."

Whose voice is it?

Many of us carry a harsh critic inside. It's the voice that points out mistakes, digs up shortcomings and says, "That's why you can't." It's built from things we heard as children, the high standards we set for ourselves, and years of comparing ourselves to others.

The trouble starts when we mistake this voice for other people's thoughts. Shining our inner criticism onto someone else's eyes is self-critical projection.

Common signs of self-critical projection

  • When a reply is slow, your first thought is "What did I do wrong?"
  • When you're praised, you think "They're just being nice."
  • When you hear laughter at a gathering, it feels like they're laughing at you.
  • One small mistake leads you to conclude "Everyone must think I'm pathetic."

It can look like modesty or social awareness, but in fact you are inventing thoughts the other person never had.

Three ways to step back from the critic

1. Give the voice a name

"There goes the critic again." When you see the thought as a habit rather than your own voice, it pulls you around less. This is what logotherapy calls self-distancing.

2. Check the evidence

Write down only what the other person actually said and did. Did anyone actually say "you're not good enough"? Most of the time, no one did.

3. Turn your attention outward

The longer you hold on to "What did I do wrong?", the bigger the thought grows. Turn your attention to the task at hand or the person in front of you. Logotherapy calls this dereflection. Self-blame shrinks faster than you'd expect.

The critic was once trying to protect you

Your inner critic began as a voice meant to protect you from mistakes and rejection. So rather than fighting to silence it, try saying, "Thanks, but I've got this one." The quieter the critic gets, the more clearly you'll hear what other people actually say.
