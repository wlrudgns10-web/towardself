The Self and Individuation: The Path Toward Yourself

Jung believed our lifelong task is not to become someone else, but to become fully ourselves.

"I" and "the Self" are different

Jung made a distinction between two things when describing the mind.

  • The ego: the center of consciousness, what we usually feel as "I." It's the me I know.
  • The Self: the center of the whole psyche, both conscious and unconscious. It's the whole me, including the parts I don't know yet.

If the ego is a small island, the Self is closer to the whole picture, including the sea that holds the island.

What is individuation?

Individuation is the process of the ego widening toward the Self. It doesn't mean standing out from everyone else. It means recognizing and accepting the many parts of ourselves and becoming one undivided person.

What we meet along the way

  1. Stepping back from the persona: knowing that our social roles are not all we are
  2. Facing the shadow: recognizing and accepting the parts we pushed away. Jung saw this as the first gate of individuation.
  3. Talking with the anima/animus: growing in ourselves the opposite side we used to project onto others
  4. Experiencing the Self: the sense that all of this comes together. Jung said this is not something finished in one go, but a journey that lasts a lifetime.

The Whisperling Test and individuation

When we project less of our shadow onto others, we not only see them more as they are, we also see ourselves more fully, because we realize that what we saw in them was also in us. That's why noticing projection is a first step toward individuation. The name of this site, "toward self," comes from this idea too.

Something to try today

Before you fall asleep, ask yourself: "Which side of me did I show today, and which side did I hide?" It's fine if no answer comes right away. Asking is where individuation begins.
