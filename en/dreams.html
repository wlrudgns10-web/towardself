Finding Your Shadow in Dream Figures

The person chasing you in a dream, the stranger you couldn't stop thinking about. Who were they?

How Jung saw dreams

Jung saw dreams as letters from the unconscious to the conscious mind. And he believed dreams try to show rather than to hide: they put into images the sides of ourselves we don't usually see and the places where we've lost balance. So instead of looking up meanings in a dream dictionary, he paid attention to how a dream made the dreamer feel.

Shadow figures in dreams

Jung thought that uncomfortable or unfamiliar figures of the same gender as the dreamer often stand for the shadow. For example:

  • A threatening person chasing you
  • Someone rude or reckless, the opposite of how you usually are
  • Someone who strangely makes you feel contempt or shame
  • Or the reverse: someone glowing with a confidence you don't have

If you're running from or fighting that figure in the dream, it may be a sign that you're strongly pushing that trait away in waking life too.

Four steps to find your shadow through dreams

  1. Write it down right away: as soon as you wake up, jot down the scenes and people you remember, even briefly. Dreams fade fast.
  2. Describe the figure in three words: describe the most striking person in three words, for example: loud, shameless, free.
  3. Compare with yourself: see how different those three words are from your usual self. The more opposite, the more likely it's your shadow.
  4. Connect it to waking life: think about whether you've recently reacted strongly to someone with those traits. Dreams and projection are often telling the same story.

A word of caution

A dream is just one clue. Rather than drawing conclusions from a dream or two, it's better to watch for figures or scenes that repeat. If frightening or painful dreams keep coming, talk with a professional instead of interpreting them alone.
