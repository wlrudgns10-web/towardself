Emotions Are Information

Emotions aren't broken alarms. We just need to read carefully what the alarm is about.

Two common mistakes with emotions

  • Suppressing: we push feelings away with "I shouldn't get angry" or "Feeling hurt means I'm petty." Pushed-away feelings don't disappear. They become shadow and burst out somewhere else.
  • Being swept away: we believe the feeling is the fact. If we feel hurt, the other person must have ignored us. If we feel anxious, there must be real danger.

What both mistakes have in common is that we don't read the feeling.

What emotions tell us

EmotionWhat it often signalsA question to ask yourself
AngerSomething precious was crossedWhat did I want to protect?
HurtAn expectation wasn't metWhat did I expect, and did I say it?
AnxietyThere's something I'm afraid of losingWhat am I afraid to lose?
EnvyThere's something I want tooWhat haven't I allowed myself?
ShameI feel I fell short of a standardWhose standard is it?

Notice that most of what emotions tell us is about ourselves rather than the other person. Anger is less proof that someone is bad and more a signal of what we hold dear.

Three steps to read emotions as information

  1. Name it: "Right now, I feel hurt." Research suggests that simply putting an accurate name to a feeling can lower its intensity.
  2. Find the message: use the questions in the table above to find what this feeling is telling you.
  3. Choose an action: once you know the message, choose what to do instead of being dragged along. If the hurt was saying "I want to spend time together," you can say "Let's have dinner this weekend" instead of blaming.
