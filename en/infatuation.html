Why Do the Rose-Colored Glasses Come Off?

At first, even their smallest words felt special. Now the same words barely register.

What the rose-colored glasses really are

When we fall in love, the other person looks more wonderful and perfect than they really are. From Jung's point of view, much of this glow comes from shining what we wish for onto the other person: idealizing projection. The pull gets even stronger when we project our less-developed opposite side, the anima or animus.

When the glasses come off

Over time, the ordinary person hidden behind the projection starts to show. They get irritable when tired, forget plans, and react differently than we expected. At this point, many people feel "they've changed."

But most of the time, they haven't changed. The light we were shining on them has faded.

The fork in the road after disappointment

  • Finding new glasses: end the disappointing relationship and look for someone who seems perfect again. If this keeps repeating, only the person changes while the same disappointment returns.
  • Trying to change them: demand that they go back to how they first seemed. They're being asked to be someone they never were, and both people wear out.
  • Starting a real meeting: get to know the person behind the projection as if meeting them for the first time. Lasting relationships usually begin here.

Practices for a lasting relationship

  1. Separate facts from wishes: write down "what I've actually seen" and "what I wished for" about your partner.
  2. Make the wish your own: pick one quality you wished for in them and grow it yourself. If you loved their easygoing nature, try leaving some open time in your own schedule.
  3. Appreciate the ordinary: find one thing they did for you on a regular day, not a special moment, and tell them.
