How to Keep a 5-Minute Daily Review Journal

Long journals rarely last three days. Four questions and five minutes are enough.

Why review your day?

Projection happens in an instant, so it's hard to notice in the moment. But when you look back calmly a day later, you can see "I reacted way too strongly back then." As these reviews pile up, you start to notice even in the moment.

Four questions

  1. When was I most shaken today?
  2. What did my Whisperling tell me? (the words that flashed through my head, exactly as they came)
  3. What actually happened? (who said what, facts only)
  4. What was one meaningful moment today?

Sample entry

1. When I was shaken: I posted an idea in the team chat and nobody replied.

2. What my Whisperling said: "Everyone just skims past your ideas. You shouldn't have posted."

3. What actually happened: I posted at 3 p.m., and nobody replied until 6. Everyone was in an outside meeting during that time.

4. A meaningful moment: I laughed for a long time on the phone with a friend on my way home.

Writing #2 and #3 side by side shows at a glance how much you added. This is the practice of treating your first thought as a guess.

Tips for keeping it going

  • Set a time: attach it to something you already do, like right after brushing your teeth or just before bed, and you won't forget.
  • Keep it short: one line per question is plenty. Trying to write a lot wears you out fast.
  • Don't pass judgment: don't argue about who was wrong. Just note how you felt at the time.
  • Skip empty days: if nothing shook you, writing only #4 is fine.
  • Reread once a week: if the same situations or people keep showing up, that's a clue pointing to your shadow.

How often?

If your Whisperling Volume came out loud, try it every day for two weeks. If it was in the middle, about three times a week is enough. Take the test again after two weeks to see what's changed.
