Why Does That One Person Make Me So Angry?

When others do the same thing, it's fine. But when that one person does it, you can't stand it.

What the size of a feeling tells you

Getting angry at someone is natural, and some people really are rude. But if the feeling is far bigger than the situation, or you react strongly to the same behavior only from one particular person, that feeling probably carries information about your own mind as well as about them.

Three things that often get mixed in

1. A side of yourself you've forbidden

For someone who has sworn "I will never be that lazy," a coworker who takes it easy becomes unbearable. Often that anger comes from having pressed down your own wish to rest for a long time. This is what Jung called the shadow.

2. Someone from the past

Sometimes a person's tone or expression reminds us of someone who once made life hard for us. Then we end up reacting to the person in our memory more than to the one in front of us.

3. Envy

When someone freely enjoys what we want but haven't allowed ourselves, such as speaking up or living freely, envy often shows up wearing the face of anger.

Questions to ask when you're angry

  1. Exactly which behavior of this person bothers you? Write it in one word.
  2. If someone else did the same thing, would you be this angry?
  3. Is it something you've forbidden yourself or held back in your own life?
  4. Does this person remind you of someone?

Answering these questions won't make the anger vanish right away. But once you see that part of the feeling is your own story, your anger toward that person often eases noticeably.

If there really is a problem

Recognizing projection doesn't erase the other person's mistakes. But once you've separated your feeling from what they actually did, you can speak far more calmly and precisely. Instead of "You're just selfish," you might say, "It put me in a tough spot when you sent the meeting files right before the deadline."
