Anima and Animus, Made Simple

If someone who once seemed perfect suddenly looks ordinary, did they change, or did what you were seeing change?

Jung's "other side within"

Jung said a man's mind holds a feminine side, which he called the anima, and a woman's mind holds a masculine side, the animus. Both words come from Latin terms for "soul" and "spirit."

In Jung's time, the early 20th century, people strongly believed personality was set by gender. So today the idea is usually understood more broadly, regardless of gender, as "the opposite side of myself that I haven't developed much." The Whisperling Test reads it this way too.

Attraction like this is natural, and it can be what gets a relationship started. The trouble begins when we see the other person less as a person and more as someone who will fill in our missing side.

The cycle of idealization and disappointment

When we project our opposite side onto someone, they look far more special and perfect than they really are. Over time, though, their ordinary and clumsy sides start to show. That's when the disappointment arrives: "They've changed," or "I was fooled."

In truth, they were probably like that from the start. What changed is that the light we were shining on them faded. If this keeps happening across relationships, it may be time to look at what we were projecting rather than trying to change the other person.

Practices for taking back the projection

  1. Separate facts from wishes: about someone you're drawn to, write two lists: "what I've actually seen" and "what I wish they were."
  2. Make the wish your own: pick one wish and turn it into a small action you can grow in yourself. If you envied their decisiveness, try making one small decision on your own today.
  3. See a person as a person: remind yourself that even someone who seems wonderful has ordinary days and awkward moments. You can only truly get close once they step down from the pedestal.
