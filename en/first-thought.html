Your First Thought Is a Guess, Not a Fact

You sent a friend a long message about something on your mind. The reply: "k." What was the first thought that crossed your mind?

Words are short; interpretations are long

What people say always has blanks. "Let's talk about this tomorrow" comes with no reason. "You look tired" comes with only a tone of voice. Our brains can't leave these blanks empty, so they fill them in fast. The problem is that we often fill them with material from our own minds rather than information from the other person.

Psychologists have studied this tendency under several names: hostile attribution bias, reading ambiguous behavior as hostile intent, and mind reading, believing we know what others think without checking. These tendencies grow stronger when we're tired or anxious, and when our projection runs strong.

How distortion happens

What was saidWhat we addedWhat we can actually know
"k"They found my message annoyingThey read the message
"Should we consider other options?"My idea was badThey suggested looking at more options
(No replies in the group chat)Everyone finds me awkwardNo one has replied yet

Notice how much narrower the third column is than you might think. Most of what we feel sure about comes from the second column.

Three ways to handle a first thought

1. Label it

When you feel shaken, say to yourself before reacting: "I'm having the thought that I was ignored." The moment you label a thought, a small gap opens between you and it.

2. Make three interpretations

Come up with one negative, one neutral and one positive reading of the same words. For "k": "they were annoyed," "they were busy," "they just reply briefly when they agree." This practice shows you that your first thought is only one of three.

3. If it matters, ask

If it's important to the relationship, stop guessing and check briefly: "Hey, did you mean this by what you said earlier?" Most misunderstandings are cleared up by that one sentence.

First thoughts arrive on their own. But whether to believe them is up to you.
